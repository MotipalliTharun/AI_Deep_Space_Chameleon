1. Executive summary
AI Space Chameleon is a one-line embeddable support widget that adapts to any
website's look, answers customers with RAG, runs multi-step requests through a data-
structure-based task engine ("AI Stacks and Queues"), and hands the chat to a human
agent with full context when it cannot finish. The MVP launches on Feb 1, 2027, 17 weeks
after kickoff, with a 7-person team and targets small and mid-sized online stores first.

The problem. Support teams are overloaded with repeat questions, and today's AI agents
are either expensive per-resolution add-ons locked to one helpdesk (Fin at $0.99,
Zendesk at $1.50 to $2.00, Gorgias at $0.90 to $1.00 per resolution) or simple FAQ bots that
cannot carry out multi-step tasks reliably. Customers also distrust bots that trap them: a
Gartner study found 64% of customers would prefer companies not use AI for service.

The bet. Three things together are the wedge:

 1. Chameleon widget: one <script> tag, auto-themes to the host site, works on any
     stack (Shopify, WordPress, React, plain HTML).
 2. AI Stacks and Queues: every request becomes a structured task. A priority queue
    orders work, a stack breaks a request into ordered sub-steps with rollback, and a
    dependency graph runs independent steps in parallel. The result is predictable,
    auditable execution instead of one opaque LLM call.
 3. Honest handoff: when confidence drops, a step fails, or the customer asks, the task
    moves to a priority queue of human agents with a summary, the step trail and
    suggested next action. Customers are never trapped.

2. Market research
The market is large and growing about 20% a year, but pricing is the top buyer complaint:
per-resolution fees make the best months the most expensive, and most AI agents only
work inside their own helpdesk. That leaves room for a flat-priced, helpdesk-neutral
widget that executes tasks reliably.


Market size
     Grand View Research puts the global chatbot market at $9.6B in 2025 and $11.8B in
     2026, reaching $41.2B by 2033 (19.6% CAGR). Customer service is the largest
     application and retail/e-commerce the largest vertical. (source)
     MarketsandMarkets sizes the wider AI-for-customer-service market at $12.06B in
     2024, rising to $47.82B by 2030 (25.8% CAGR). (source)
     Gartner predicts agentic AI will resolve 80% of common service issues without a
     human by 2029, cutting operating costs about 30%. (source)
     The counterweight: Gartner also predicts half of companies that cut service staff
     because of AI will rehire by 2027, and only 20% of service leaders surveyed had
     actually reduced staff. Human handoff stays essential. (source)

Serviceable market for the MVP (estimate, to validate in discovery): English-speaking
online stores and SaaS companies with 2 to 50 support agents that run their own website.
These buyers want AI now but balk at enterprise suites.

Gaps and positioning
 1. Predictable price. Flat tiers by conversation volume, never per resolution. The
    customer is not punished for success.
 2. Helpdesk-neutral. Hand off to Zendesk, Freshdesk, Gorgias, Slack or our own
     lightweight inbox.
 3. Reliable multi-step tasks. The task engine shows every step, retries safely, and rolls
     back half-finished actions. Competitors mostly expose actions as single tool calls.
 4. Looks native. Auto-theming removes the design work that makes most widgets look
    bolted on.
 5. Trust by design. Clear AI disclosure, a "talk to a human" button that always works, and
    visible sources on every answer.

Positioning line: "The support widget that blends into your site, finishes the job, and
knows when to call a human."



3. Product vision and concept
The product has two halves: the Chameleon widget that customers see, and the AI
Stacks and Queues engine that turns each request into a structured, trackable task.
Everything the AI does is a task with steps, a state and an owner, so it can be paused,

retried, audited or handed to a person.


The Chameleon widget
     One-line install: <script src="https://cdn.spacechameleon.ai/w.js" data-
     site="abc123"></script> , plus plugins for Shopify and WordPress.
     Auto-theming: on load it reads the host page's computed colors, fonts and corner
     radius and builds matching design tokens. The site owner can lock or override them in
     the dashboard.
     Isolation: renders inside Shadow DOM so host CSS cannot break it and it cannot break
     the host.
     Small and fast: loader under 15 KB gzipped; the full chat bundle loads lazily when the
     bubble is first opened.
     Context aware: knows the current page, cart and logged-in customer (through a
     signed token from the host), so answers fit the moment.


AI Stacks and Queues: how a request runs
Each customer message is classified into an intent. Simple questions are answered
directly with RAG. Anything that needs actions ("change my delivery address", "return
item 2 and exchange for size M") becomes a Task that the engine plans and executes step
by step.

Worked example: "Return the blue shoes and send size 9 instead." The planner produces
four steps: verify order ownership, create return, check size-9 stock, create exchange
order. Steps 1 then 2 run in order; stock check runs in parallel with step 2. If stock is out at
step 3, the engine pops the undo stack (cancels the return label) and offers the customer
a refund or a human. The customer sees a progress card with each step ticked off.

SC-12 Order status. As a logged-in customer, I want to ask "where is my order?" so I get
tracking without waiting.

     Given one open order, when I ask, then I see order number, status and tracking link.
     Given several open orders, when I ask, then the widget asks which one.
     Given I am not logged in, when I ask, then it asks for order number and email and

     Given the new size is in stock, when I confirm, then a return label and exchange order

     Given the new size is out of stock, when the check fails, then the return label is

     Given an escalated chat, when I open it, then I see a 3-line summary, the steps

SC-40 Auto-theme. As a store owner, I want the widget to match my brand without design
work.

     Given my site's primary color and font, when the widget loads, then its bubble, header
     and buttons use them and pass AA contrast.

7. Architecture and tech stack
One multi-tenant backend serves every site: the router sends plain questions to RAG,
action requests to the task engine, and "human please" straight to the escalation queue.
The task engine is the differentiator; its queues live in Redis and its task records in
PostgreSQL so nothing is lost on restart.

8. AI-to-human escalation design
The AI hands over whenever it cannot finish safely, and the customer never has to repeat
themselves: the agent gets the summary, the steps already done and a suggested reply.
Escalations with no outcome are free to the tenant, so we are never paid to keep
customers away from humans.


When the AI escalates

  human

How a handoff runs
 1. The engine freezes the task and writes a handoff packet: 3-line summary, intent,
     customer details the tenant allows, completed and rolled-back steps, failure reason,
     suggested reply.
 2. The packet enters the escalation priority queue for the right skill group. Order =
     priority first, then time waiting.




                                                                                                      Page 13 of 22
AI Space Chameleon — Project Plan




 3. The widget tells the customer honestly: "I'm connecting you to a person. Current wait
     is about 2 minutes." The position updates live.
 4. Agent available: the first free agent in that group claims the chat (claim is atomic, so
     two agents never take the same chat). The agent sees the packet and replies in the
     same widget thread.
 5. No agent online / after hours: the customer can leave an email; the chat becomes a
     ticket in the built-in inbox or Zendesk with the packet attached, and the customer gets
     a reference number.
 6. Wait too long: if the wait passes the tenant's SLA (default 5 minutes), the item gains
     priority and the lead is notified.
 7. After the human resolves it, the agent marks "AI should have handled this" or "needed
     a human". Those labels retune thresholds and add eval cases (section 6).


Agent experience
     Inbox with queues per skill, wait timers and priority badges.
     One-click "send suggested reply", editable before sending.
     "Hand back to AI" for the easy tail of a chat (for example, sending a tracking link after
     the agent approved an exception).
     Internal notes and transfer to another group with the packet kept.

11. Business model, go-to-market and KPIs
Flat monthly tiers by conversation volume, with unlimited agent seats and no per-
resolution fee, undercut per-outcome competitors as soon as a store automates a few
hundred chats a month. Prices below are proposals to test with beta stores.


Proposed pricing

  Plan                  Price /     AI              Includes
                        month       conversations

  Free                  $0          100             Widget, RAG answers, "Powered by" badge

  Starter               $49         1,000           No badge, 1 action, agent inbox

  Growth                $199        5,000           All actions, Zendesk handoff, analytics,
                                                    feedback inbox

  Scale                 $599        20,000          Priority support, SSO, custom actions via
                                                    webhook

  Enterprise            Custom      Custom          SLA, data residency, dedicated model
                                                    options


Overage: $15 per extra 1,000 conversations, with alerts at 80% and 100% and a hard cap
the owner controls. The widget never goes dark: past the cap it falls back to human
handoff.

Comparison point: a store with 2,000 AI resolutions a month pays about $1,980 in Fin
outcome fees alone at $0.99 each, before seats (source). The same volume fits our $199
Growth plan.

Unit economics to prove in the PoC: LLM + infrastructure cost per conversation must
stay under $0.03 so Growth keeps a gross margin above 70%. Levers: small model for
routing, LRU answer cache, short prompts, larger model only for planning.
